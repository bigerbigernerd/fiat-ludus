Compiled 2026-11-12. All dates are UTC, taken from the post data (`created_at`). Every post below was confirmed without logging in, using the public syndication endpoint (`cdn.syndication.twimg.com/tweet-result`, which gives the full JSON, likes and media) and the oEmbed endpoint (`publish.twitter.com/oembed`). Both endpoints worked for all items. Like counts are as of 2026-11-12. Quotes are copied from the confirmed text; for long posts the syndication text is cut off near 280 characters, so quotes come only from the visible part. "Pick" = recommended for the blog; each pick has a screenshot in `screens/`. These are real X embeds rendered by widgets.js in headless Chromium at 2x scale, light theme. The timestamp printed on a screenshot is in the renderer's local time zone, so it can differ by a day from the UTC date in the table. Four screenshots (levelsio, McGreenBeats, notjazii, kevin_t_ngo) also show the post each one quotes, under the main post. In the notjazii screenshot, the quoted video thumbnail rendered grey; the main post is unaffected.

## What this adds up to

The confirmed posts show the "one prompt → playable 3D browser game" demo turning into a standard test for each new model in 2026. Each release has its own wave: Claude Opus 5 in late July (Shumer, then a dozen re-runs and comparisons), GPT-6 Astra in early September, the Gemini 4 Carbon arena checkpoint in late October, DeepSeek V4.1-Flash-1017 in mid-October, and Claude Fable 5.2 on 2026-11-09. The quality bar visibly rises along the way: from procedural three.js scenes to games with full HUDs, AAA-styled lighting and generated audio, which people at the labs play with each other. Cost also became part of the story. Rieseberg's Fable 5.2 shooter is the only high-reach post that reports dollars, tokens and runtime (~$550, 8 h). At the other end, several DeepSeek V4.1 Flash posts report a single game for $0.30–$0.80, though from low-reach accounts and without independent checks. The posts do not show a representative success rate: they are a selected sample of best outputs, and none reports failed attempts. "One-shot" often hides a lot of help. Sholto Douglas wrote a detailed design brief, Shumer's repo uses a critic loop, Altucher's re-run took over ten hours, and Kevin Ngo's in-game music came from Suno. Several Astra demos relied on image generation, Meshy or ElevenLabs. Almost none of these games has been tested by players beyond the author's friends. The pushback is real and engaged: Kenney's post (16.9K likes) and Tim Sweeney's reply, GameMakers' "ONE PROMPT. FULL BUILD. BULL SHIT.", and a publisher cancelling a demo over fear of AI clones. The posts that best make the case are: @felixrieseberg (concrete cost and runtime, playable link, lab author); @mattshumer_ (the July post that started the wave, with a public repo and prompt); @_sholtodouglas (a researcher calls it "seriously fun" and admits the detailed prompt in a reply); and @notjazii or @quimedesu (the cheap-model cost contrast that motivates our stack).
